### Overall cycling experience

I rented the bike from 9am, returned at 5pm. Cycling in Sydney can be quite stressful, as I said above. And also, there are a lot of uphills and downhills, wow Sydney is so hilly. I would not recommend it if you are not experienced. Also, if you want a nice coastal view along the cycling path, Sydney isn’t the place. Melbourne did a somewhat better job.

## Goodbye

![](./image77.jpg)

Final expensive Australian meal of spicy chicken rolls, against the runways backdrop at the airport.

The trip has been thoroughly enjoyable! I booked the experiences that I wanted, steering away from the “ideal” itineraries found online. It can be more expensive than the free tourist attractions, of course. But that gave me so much joy in the activities that I paid for. Sydney Helitours was a highlight: I love being on that helicopter and looking at the city from a height, though it was more on the expensive side.

There are places nearby Melbourne and Sydney to visit, and would arguably require a car to travel to, since there is no public transport to these places. I would argue otherwise: you can still book day tours, and these tours are at a very affordable price.

Daily cost here can be quite high: food in restaurants is 20-30 AUD on average, or 15-25 AUD in food courts. Transport is also quite expensive, nothing can be so highly functional yet still so cheap like Singapore public transport.

People here are quite nice and friendly, every staff member at stores and restaurants will ask ‘how are you’, some occasionally reach out to you to see if you need help. Aussie accent is also somewhat easy to pick up if you have made jokes about British accent before, I probably can recite the Eucharistic prayer in Aussie accent :)

Catholicism seems to be more happening here, from what I can observe: 2 out of 4 churches that I went to have Eucharistic adoration in the main hall after morning mass, and one has the priest standing at the back greeting people, even when it was weekday mass.
